Training camp daily schedule – a sample week

A good weekly plan balances workload, recovery and organisation. This example is a guide, not a fixed plan – your coaching team decides the content.

A good weekly plan balances workload, recovery and organisation. This example is a guide, not a fixed plan – your coaching team decides the content.

At a glance

  • Balance workload and recovery
  • Build transfers into the day plan
  • Allow buffer time
  • Agree the plan with the hotel beforehand
01

Arrival day

Flight, transfer, check-in and dinner. Optionally a light activation or team meeting, depending on arrival time.

02

A typical training day

Breakfast, morning session, lunch and rest, afternoon session or tactics, dinner and team time. Breaks and drinks are built in.

03

Test match day

Travel to the venue, warm-up, match, recovery and analysis. Plan transfer and catering beforehand.

04

Recovery day

A light programme with pool, gym, a walk or video analysis. It helps prevent overload.

05

Departure day

Breakfast, check-out, transfer. Check room lists and luggage in advance.

More questions and answers

How many sessions per day are typical?

Often one to two, depending on age, level and goal. Your coaching team decides.

When should the test match be?

Usually mid-week or towards the end so workload and recovery fit.

How long should a camp be?

Many clubs plan several nights up to about a week, depending on goal and budget.

Which teams do you support?

Youth teams, amateur clubs, senior teams, academies and professional squads.

Which services are possible?

Hotel, catering, pitches, recovery, test matches, airport transfer and local transport – combined to your wishes.

How does an enquiry with Antalya Camp work?

You send the form with key details; we usually reply within 24 hours with a first assessment and an offer.

Can test matches be organised?

We request suitable opponents for your dates. Confirmation depends on availability and level.
